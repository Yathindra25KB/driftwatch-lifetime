Related-work addendum (Phase 7)¶

Written 15 Oct 2026 by Claude Code for the researcher to check. It covers the “newer neighbours” of Appendix G of the implementation plan (docs/source/DriftWatch_Implementation_Plan.pdf, pp. 50 to 51), plus the items that Appendix G lists as “not re-read”. Every entry was read at the abstract level only, from the arXiv abstract page, on 2026-10-15; none of the full papers was read, so a column entry of “not in the abstract” means exactly that and is not a statement about the paper. Please read each paper before citing it. Descriptions are my paraphrase of the abstract.

The guide’s own related-work table (section 4) stands and is not repeated here.

How to read the table¶

Three axes of this study, as in the guide:

  • Delay: does the work treat the time a requested label takes to arrive as a parameter?

  • Budget: does it fix or study the number of labels an analyst can buy?

  • Conformal or monitor: does it use conformal p-values, or a drift monitor, as a signal?

“Not in the abstract” is not “no”.

Minnei, Manca, Piras, Sotgiu, Pintor, Ghiani, Maiorca, Giacinto, Biggio. Label-efficient Training Updates for Malware Detection over Time, 2603.28396 (30 Mar 2026)

A model-agnostic framework that tests active and semi-supervised learning, alone and combined, on Android and Windows malware; reports annotation cost cut by up to 90% at detection performance near full-label retraining; adds a feature-level drift analysis that relates feature stability to detector performance

Not in the abstract

Central: annotation cost is the objective, though a fixed per-slot budget is not described

Feature-level drift analysis; no conformal in the abstract

The closest neighbour on budget and on the two platforms. This study fixes the budget per slot, makes label arrival a parameter, and reports A-AUT. Compare after reading how its time slices and its labelling cost are defined

Herzog, Eusebi, Cavallaro. On the Reliability and Stability of Selective Methods in Malware Classification Tasks (Aurora), 2505.22843 (28 May 2025, revised 21 Jan 2026)

Asks whether drift-adaptive malware classifiers hold up in operation beyond standard metrics; Aurora checks whether confidence estimates are trustworthy and performance is stable over time; finds current approaches fragile across datasets with different drift

Not in the abstract

Motivation: unreliable confidence wastes annotation budgets in active learning

Confidence-error alignment and selective classification; no conformal in the abstract

Relevant to RQ3: selection by confidence is only as good as the confidence. Cite as a caution about the credibility and uncertainty selectors

Kalny, Jurecek, Stamp. Detecting Concept Drift in Evolving Malware Families Using Rule-Based Classifier Representations, 2604.22629 (24 Apr 2026)

Structural drift detection from rule sets of decision trees trained across temporal windows on EMBER2024; fixed two-month windows with feature-level Pearson correlation worked most reliably; no single method dominates; baselines include RIPPER and Transcendent

Not in the abstract

Not in the abstract

Drift monitoring (structure-based)

A monitor-side neighbour for RQ4 and for the EMBER2024 tier. Its window finding is relevant to the slot-length question (ADR-0004)

Li and Bertino. LFreeDA: Label-Free Drift Adaptation for Windows Malware Detection, 2511.14963 (18 Nov 2025)

Label-free adaptation: unsupervised domain adaptation on malware images generates and filters pseudo-labels, then a control-flow-graph classifier is adapted; reports up to 12.6% accuracy and 11.1% F1 gains over no adaptation, near methods given 300 true labels

Not in the abstract

Label-free by design

Avoids drift detection by design

The zero-label end of the budget axis, for Windows. A reference point for “what a budget of zero buys” if a pseudo-label arm is ever added; not used in this study

Alam, Piplai, Rastogi. ADAPT: A Pseudo-labeling Approach to Combat Concept Drift in Malware Detection, 2507.08597 (11 Jul 2025, revised 3 Aug 2025)

Model-agnostic semi-supervised pseudo-labelling to use unlabeled data under drift; works with neural and tree models; five datasets across Android, Windows and PDF

Not in the abstract

Only indirectly (avoids ground-truth labels)

Not in the abstract

Same family as LFreeDA: adaptation without true labels. Context for the statement that true labels are the scarce resource

Zheng, Yang, Ngai, Jana, Cavallaro. TIF: Learning Temporal Invariance in Android Malware Detectors, 2502.05098 (7 Feb 2025, revised 22 Jun 2026)

A training framework that groups data into environments by observation date and uses contrastive learning with invariant gradient alignment so that detectors generalise across time; reports gains especially in early deployment

Not in the abstract

Not in the abstract

Not in the abstract

A way to slow decay by training rather than by labelling. Orthogonal to the budget and delay axes; a possible future model arm

Roh, Kaya, Kruegel, Vigna, Hong. MADCAT: Combating Malware Detection Under Concept Drift with Test-Time Adaptation, 2505.18734 (24 May 2025)

Self-supervised encoder-decoder; adapts at test time using a small balanced sample of test data; outperforms baselines on continuous Android malware detection

Not in the abstract

A small test-time sample is used; no labelling budget is described

Not in the abstract

Adapts at test time without true labels. Relevant to the question of what “retrain” means in the policy axis

He, Lei, Qin, Ren, Chen. Combating Concept Drift with Explanatory Detection and Adaptation for Android Malware Classification (DREAM), 2405.04095 (7 May 2024, revised 24 May 2025)

Drift detection with explanations in a contrastive autoencoder; supports concept revision for retraining; matches the best existing method on a Drebin classifier with 76.6% fewer newly labelled samples

Not in the abstract

Reduces expert effort and newly labelled samples; no fixed budget

Drift detection is central

A drift-monitor neighbour that also counts labels. Place beside the monitors of RQ4

Kamol, Lopez, Nowmi, Rivas, Haque, Raff, Piplai, Rahman. McNdroid: A Longitudinal Multimodal Benchmark for Robust Drift Detection in Android Malware, 2605.06894 (7 May 2026)

A 2013 to 2025 (without 2015) Android benchmark with static, dynamic and call-graph features; degradation over growing train-test gaps; multimodal fusion beats the best single modality over long gaps

Not in the abstract

Not in the abstract

Drift analysis and benchmarking; a monitor is not described in the abstract

A dataset neighbour of LAMDA (the same years and gap). Would be a third Android source if licences and size allow; not used

Yu, Chen, Zhang, Liang, Qiao, Lai. EMA: Efficient Model Adaptation for Learning-based Systems, 2605.13942 (13 May 2026)

Adapts models in networked systems with state transformers for a warm start and by prioritising which data to label; 14.9% to 42.4% lower adaptation cost across eight systems

Not in the abstract

Trade-off between labelling and training cost; no fixed labelling budget

Not as a monitor

A systems paper, not malware. Listed in the plan because it prioritises what to label; probably not a neighbour for citation. Kept as a note

Not identified¶

  • “Li et al. (NDSS 2025)” appears in Table G.1 of the plan without a title. One web search on 2026-10-15 did not find an NDSS 2025 paper by an author named Li on malware drift. It may be the Li and Bertino paper above (LFreeDA is arXiv only, not NDSS) or a different work. A second search on 2026-10-20 found one candidate: A. S. Li, A. Iyengar, A. Kundu and E. Bertino, “Revisiting Concept Drift in Windows Malware Detection: Adaptation to Real Drifted Malware with Minimal Samples”, NDSS 2025 (arXiv 2407.13918; https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/revisiting-concept-drift-in-windows-malware-detection-adaptation-to-real-drifted-malware-with-minimal-samples/). It argues that active-learning retraining is suboptimal and proposes domain adaptation on control-flow graphs. It fits the context but has not been matched to Table G.1. On 2026-10-22 the paper was confirmed on the NDSS 2025 accepted-papers list (ndss-symposium.org/ndss2025/accepted-papers/) with the same title and the same four authors, and the arXiv record 2407.13918 (v1 18 Jul 2024, updated 19 Dec 2024) gives the same title and authors [L-33]. It is in paper/references.bib as li2024revisiting. [verify: the researcher confirms that this is the paper Table G.1 means by “Li et al. (NDSS 2025)”.]

  • ActDroid (arXiv 2401.16982), SEED (arXiv 2605.24903), FIRCE (arXiv 2605.01962), arXiv 2606.07789 are in the guide’s table and are covered in report/ch05_related_work.md; they were not re-read for this addendum.

Position, in the words the guide allows¶

No neighbour read at abstract level combines label delay as a parameter, a fixed analyst budget and conformal drift monitoring, reported with A-AUT on Android and Windows PE data. That means “not found in a limited search”, not “absent”: the search was one session of abstract-level reading and can miss papers whose abstracts do not use these words.

Contribution statement (the guide’s wording): “We study malware detector lifetime under label delay, a fixed analyst budget and conformal drift monitoring in a single time-aware protocol, reported with A-AUT, on Android and Windows PE data.” The word “first” is not used.

What changes in the report¶

  • report/ch05_related_work.md: add Minnei et al. as the closest budget neighbour, and Aurora next to the selection discussion; list the rest in one sentence each.

  • E3 (Phase 8) discussion: Aurora’s caution about confidence under drift applies to the uncertainty selector and to credibility.

Audit 2026-10-27 (first monthly audit)¶

Five are recorded below, three are adjacent but outside this study and are set aside, and 41 are keyword matches from unrelated fields (biomedicine, robotics, aerospace, language models). “Not in the abstract” is not “no”.
