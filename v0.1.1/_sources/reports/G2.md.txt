## Open decision A: the RQ1 comparator

The same fitted comparator model scored two ways (CLAIMS E-19; E1's window table reproduces E-02 and E-03 exactly):

| | E0 window-level comparator | E1 per-slot comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Test rows | all held-out rows of the pooled window, including the training-era slots | held-out rows of the evaluation slots only |
| C3 | one malware cap over the pool | per slot, checked |
| Aggregation | F1 pooled over rows, minus AUT | per-slot F1, then AUT, per window |
| LAMDA gap (LR; LightGBM) | 0.152; 0.137 | 0.029; 0.040 |
| BODMAS gap (LR; LightGBM) | 0.028; -0.000 | 0.035; 0.006 |

Steps between the two LAMDA LR numbers (E-19): pooled F1 over all held-out rows 0.831; over evaluation-slot rows only, 0.727; mean of per-slot F1, 0.708; time-aware AUT, 0.679. The part due to the training-era rows and the part due to the single global C3 draw are not separated, because the comparator's predictions were not stored in E0.

What the E1 comparator does not yet show cleanly (E-18): its slots reach a lower ratio than the time-aware slots on LAMDA (0.075 against 0.084) because the random draw removes proportionally more malware than benign from slots near the target. On the cells where both series reached the target the per-slot gap is larger (LAMDA 0.062 LR, 0.042 LightGBM). So the E1 gap is probably biased downward and the E0 gap upward; neither is a "leakage-only" quantity.

What can be said: the gap depends strongly on the definition, the pre-registered band is passed under one definition and not under the other on LAMDA, and both were seen before the tag. What cannot be said: that the gap is "mostly temporal leakage", or that it is large or small. The choice of the primary comparator is the researcher's. It should be written into the PREREG RQ1 row and an ADR as made after seeing both, with the definitional reason (a comparator matched per slot is the one that matches "same data, same C3 ratio"), and both numbers reported. A stratified within-slot hold-out (each slot loses the same share of malware and benign) would remove the ratio depletion; that would be a new comparator, an ADR and a rerun. G1's criterion 1 ("gap of at least 0.05 on one dataset") is comparator-dependent: met on E0's definition, not on E1's mean gap.

## Open decision B: memory and parallelism

The memory watchdog measures the process, so concurrent runs add up. Two BODMAS arms in two threads were failed by the watchdog (E-20); one at a time they fit. The BODMAS E1 run came within 27 MiB of the cap (1.47 GiB of 1.5 GiB). Choices for the researcher: run the grid sequentially (default `--workers 1`), shrink BODMAS runs (the shrink order in PREREG: delay 2, budget 0.5%, windows, seeds), or raise the cap with the knowledge that the machine has 7.25 GB. I have not changed the cap.

## What the label-influence diagnostic does and does not say

At 0.5% and 5% of the slot, after the first refit, retraining changes the 0.5 decision on 0.88% and 2.11% of LAMDA rows for LR and 0.71% and 0.99% for LightGBM; on BODMAS 0.22% and 0.45% (LR) and 0.19% and 0.22% (LightGBM) (E-13, E-14). Among malware rows on LAMDA at 5%: 7.7% (LR) and 4.4% (LightGBM) (E-15). These are counts of decision changes. There is no noise-floor arm, no split into fixed and introduced errors, no AUT. ADR-0007 therefore records the design as chosen (weight 1, no truncation), reports the counts, and does not use the word "modest". Whether weight 1 is enough to make the budget axis informative is answered by the grid, not by this table.

## Other caveats the researcher should weigh
1. **BODMAS delay axis.** With nE = 6 and period 3 there is one useful refit; at d = 3 periodic equals never (tested), at d = 2 only slot-0 labels have arrived. Delay contrasts on BODMAS are limited by ADR-0005's geometry.
2. **The BODMAS AUT alarm from G1 is still open.** E1 on BODMAS inherits it; its per-slot gaps (0.006 to 0.035) are small against an AUT of 0.91 to 0.98.
3. **Windows overlap.** LAMDA's 16 windows are roughly four independent 12-month blocks. The E1 ranges and sign counts (E-17) are descriptive; no interval is reported.
4. **Seeds are not replicates for LR.** Five seeds are five C3 draws and comparator splits.
5. **Figure positions are not time since training.** One calendar month sits at the same position in every window of a stride-aligned run; the sawtooth at positions 4, 7, 10 on LAMDA is month composition, not drift. The figure carries a note.
6. **The pool mixes C3-subsampled arrived rows and raw initial rows** (two priors); a property of the design (ADR-0007).
7. **Per-slot C3 draw keys differ** between the time-aware series (seed, slot) and the E1 comparator (seed, window, slot), which adds window-to-window noise to the gap but not bias.
8. **Duplicates.** LAMDA has 6.3% evaluated rows repeating a training-window vector (E-10). The pool does not yet carry feature-vector hashes, so repeats between arrived labels and later slots are not counted (carried item).
9. **Reruns.** Diagnostic runs were rerun after leakage-review code changes (ADR-0007 run log, E-21); the shares are identical.
10. **Parallel threads and the shared initial-model cache** are tested only on synthetic data. A cache key bound to training-row content was added after the review.
